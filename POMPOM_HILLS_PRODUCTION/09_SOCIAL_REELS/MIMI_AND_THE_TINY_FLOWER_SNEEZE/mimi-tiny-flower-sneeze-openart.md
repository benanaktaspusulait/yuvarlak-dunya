- Use the approved Mimi character image as the character reference.
- Do not generate Mimi from a written physical description.

- Create and approve the opening frame first, then use that image as the Start Frame for Frame to Video.
- Keep **Final Frame Lock OFF** unless a separately approved final-frame image is supplied.

## 2. Visual Style and Continuity

- Pompom Hills approved 3D preschool animation style.
- Soft pastel colours, matte materials, rounded forms and gentle morning light.
- Calm, warm and low-stimulation mood.
- Mimi and the flower remain in the centre 60% safe area throughout the vertical video.
- Only Mimi and one small Pompom Flower are present.
- Keep Mimi, the flower, the hill and the background visually consistent for the entire shot.

---

## 3. Opening Frame / Take Shot Prompt

Use the approved Mimi reference image.

Create a high-quality 3D preschool animation frame in the established Pompom Hills style. Mimi is standing on Flower Hill and leaning slightly towards one tiny fluffy yellow Pompom Flower with gentle curiosity. Preserve Mimi's approved character design exactly: same face, blue colour palette, long ears, body proportions, clothes and soft matte materials. The flower is small, friendly and non-human, with no face. Gentle green hill, a few simple distant plants, soft pastel sky and warm morning sunlight. Calm, uncluttered composition with generous negative space. Mimi and the flower are fully visible inside the centre safe area. Vertical 9:16 framing. No text.

---

## 4. Frame-to-Video Motion Prompt

A single continuous 15-second shot in the established Pompom Hills 3D preschool style. Preserve the approved Mimi design and the opening frame composition exactly. Keep the camera stable with only a very slow, subtle push-in; no cuts and no sudden reframing.

**00–03 seconds:** Mimi takes one small, gentle step towards the tiny yellow Pompom Flower and leans closer with quiet curiosity. Her ears and body move naturally and softly. No dialogue.

**03–06 seconds:** The single flower wiggles gently and makes one tiny, soft, playful sneeze: “A-choo!” Mimi blinks once, makes a small surprised movement and softly says, “Oh!”

**06–09 seconds:** The flower gives one even smaller sneeze: “Choo!” Mimi relaxes, smiles and gives a short, gentle giggle. Movements remain slow and subtle.

**09–13 seconds:** Without a camera cut, Mimi turns her eyes towards the viewer while remaining beside the flower. Use accurate, restrained lip sync as she warmly says: “A sneezing flower! Have you ever seen one?”

**13–15 seconds:** Mimi gives one small wave and softly says: “See you soon!” Hold the final pose briefly. End naturally without a fade generated inside the shot.

The flower must sneeze exactly twice. Mimi must speak only the written lines. Keep all facial expressions soft, friendly and readable. No exaggerated reaction, bouncing or fast motion.

---

## 5. Audio and Lip-Sync

Prepare one 15-second master audio file containing:

| Time | Audio |
|---|---|
| 00–03s | Very quiet natural morning ambience only |
| 03–06s | Soft flower “A-choo!”, followed by Mimi: “Oh!” |
| 06–09s | Smaller flower “Choo!”, followed by Mimi's gentle giggle |
| 09–13s | Mimi: “A sneezing flower! Have you ever seen one?” |
| 13–15s | Mimi: “See you soon!” |

- Use Mimi's canonical voice reference.
- Use the master audio as the audio/lip-sync reference.
- Lip-sync only Mimi's spoken words; the flower has no mouth and does not speak.
- Keep ambience very low beneath the voice and sneeze effects.
- No music, singing, narration or additional voices.
