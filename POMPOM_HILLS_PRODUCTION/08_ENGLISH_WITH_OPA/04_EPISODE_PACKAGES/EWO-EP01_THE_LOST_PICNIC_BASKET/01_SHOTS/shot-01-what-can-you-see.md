# Shot 01 — Opa's Tree Home Learning Nook

## Purpose
Introduce Opa, Arda, and Luca inside the established Pompom Hills world. The shot must work as a complete Reel with an active mini-story, while also beginning the larger picnic adventure.

## Canonical Character Lock
Use the supplied character sheets as strict visual references.

### Opa
- Opa is a soft, round, light-green owl — not a human grandfather.
- Wise old owl and mentor of Pompom Hills.
- Large warm-brown eyes.
- Round golden-yellow glasses always worn.
- Warm orange beak and three rounded orange toes on each foot.
- Cream face and belly feathers.
- Layered soft-green feathers with no sharp shapes.
- Thick knitted warm-brown scarf always worn.
- Calm, kind and patient expression.
- Wings are rounded and have no human fingers.

### Arda
- A cheerful toddler boy, visually around 2–3 years old.
- Shorter and younger-looking than Luca.
- Soft fluffy dark-brown hair, large warm-brown eyes and rosy cheeks.
- Sky-blue short-sleeved T-shirt with the small chest badge.
- Light-blue trousers with a white drawstring.
- Soft tan shoes.
- Playful, energetic and curious.

### Luca
- A preschool boy, visually around 3–4 years old.
- Clearly taller than Arda, but the difference must remain natural and modest.
- Wavy dark-brown hair, large warm-brown eyes and light freckles.
- Cream-white T-shirt with green sleeves and the mountain emblem.
- Khaki-green shorts with pockets.
- White socks with green and orange stripes.
- Orange trainers with white soles.
- Adventurous, confident and curious.

## Character Scale and Proportions
Maintain the established hierarchy from the reference sheets:
- Arda is the smallest child in this shot.
- Luca is slightly taller than Arda.
- Opa is a large, round owl and should appear noticeably taller and wider than both boys.
- Do not make Luca dramatically taller than Arda.
- Do not shrink Opa to ordinary-bird size.
- Keep all heads large, rounded and consistent with the soft preschool animation style.

## Story Summary
Arda and Luca arrive at Opa's magical tree home for a planned picnic. Opa is sitting in his open-air learning nook, reading beside his shelves and tea table. The children greet him and notice several picnic-related picture cards on a small teaching board. Opa turns the moment into a short, natural English guessing game. When the children ask to leave, Opa reaches for the picnic basket — but it is not where he left it. They begin looking around the tree-home platform together.

## Canonical Location Lock
The scene takes place at **Opa's established tree home shown in the supplied location reference**.

This is not a conventional enclosed treehouse and not a separate cottage interior. It is a magical open-air home and learning space integrated directly into the trunk of a gigantic tree.

### Required Exterior and Platform Details
- One enormous ancient tree with a thick central trunk and a huge rounded green canopy.
- A warm arched alcove carved into the trunk.
- Built-in wooden bookshelves visible inside the alcove.
- A circular raised wooden platform around the trunk.
- Short wooden steps leading up to the platform.
- Several hanging lanterns suspended from branches around the home.
- Opa's large green cushioned reading chair.
- A low round wooden tea table with a white teapot and cups.
- Small round stump seats with green cushions.
- A wooden learning board or easel beside the shelves.
- Colourful stepping stones and flowers around the base.
- Bright whimsical Pompom Hills landscape in the distance, including rolling green hills, colourful rounded trees and a winding path.
- Warm, sunny daytime lighting.

### Learning Room Interpretation
For this episode, the “Learning Room” is Opa's **open learning nook on the tree-home platform**, directly in front of the built-in bookshelves. Do not invent a separate enclosed classroom behind a door.

## Visual Style
Premium soft 3D preschool animation matching the supplied Pompom Hills character sheets and tree-home reference. Rounded plush forms, warm natural materials, bright gentle colours, expressive faces, cinematic depth, no sharp edges and no photorealism.

## Duration
45–60 seconds

## Emotional Beat
Warm arrival → playful curiosity → small discovery game → surprise → shared mission.

## Reel Structure
The shot must have its own beginning, interaction, small learning payoff and final problem reveal. It must not feel like only an introduction to the next Reel.

## Scene Flow
1. Open on a wide establishing view of Opa's enormous tree home and the colourful Pompom Hills landscape.
2. Arda and Luca follow the stepping-stone path toward the platform.
3. Arda reaches the steps first; Luca follows closely behind.
4. Opa sits in his green chair reading a brown book beside the tea table.
5. Opa hears them, closes the book and welcomes them with a raised wing.
6. The boys climb onto the platform and greet him.
7. Arda notices three simple picture cards on Opa's learning board: a basket, an apple and a blanket.
8. Opa asks the boys what they can see.
9. Luca correctly names the basket and apple; Arda excitedly identifies the blanket after a playful hint.
10. Opa praises both children without turning the scene into a formal lesson.
11. Arda reminds Opa that they are going on a picnic.
12. Opa says they need the basket, apple and blanket they just named.
13. Luca spots the apple and folded blanket beside the tea table.
14. Opa reaches toward the usual place beside the bookshelf for the picnic basket.
15. The basket is missing.
16. Opa, Arda and Luca check around the chair, stump seats and shelves while continuing to talk.
17. End when Luca notices a small clue — one loose red-and-white picnic ribbon caught near the platform steps — giving the Reel a satisfying discovery and leading into the next shot.

## Dialogue
**Arda:** Opa! We're here!

**Luca:** Hello, Opa!

**Opa:** Hello, my little explorers. You arrived just in time.

**Arda:** What are those pictures?

**Opa:** Let's see what you know. Luca, what can you see?

**Luca:** I can see a basket... and an apple.

**Opa:** Very good. And this one, Arda?

**Arda:** Umm... a towel?

**Luca:** We sit on it outside.

**Arda:** Oh! A picnic blanket!

**Opa:** Exactly. A basket, an apple and a picnic blanket.

**Arda:** We need all three for our picnic!

**Luca:** Are you ready, Opa?

**Opa:** I have the apple... and here is the blanket.

**Arda:** Where is the basket?

**Opa:** It should be beside the bookshelf.

**Luca:** It isn't there.

**Arda:** Is it behind your chair?

**Opa:** No, not here.

**Luca:** Wait! What's that near the steps?

**Arda:** A red ribbon!

**Opa:** Hmm. That ribbon came from our picnic basket.

**Luca:** Then the basket must have gone this way!

**Opa:** Good thinking. Let's investigate together.

## Learning Focus
The learning should be embedded in the story rather than presented as a lesson.

Target expressions:
- What can you see?
- I can see a basket.
- What is this?
- A picnic blanket.
- Where is the basket?
- It isn't there.
- Is it behind the chair?
- Let's investigate together.

Target vocabulary:
- basket
- apple
- picnic blanket
- chair
- bookshelf
- ribbon

## Character Acting
- Opa uses gentle wing gestures, calm head tilts and thoughtful blinks.
- Never give Opa human hands or fingers.
- Arda moves quickly, points excitedly and sometimes answers before thinking.
- Luca is adventurous but slightly more observant and controlled.
- Keep both children active throughout; no character should stand frozen while another speaks.
- During dialogue, use small listening reactions, eye movement and natural body turns.

## Camera Direction
- Begin with a cinematic wide shot matching the supplied tree-home composition.
- Gently push toward the circular platform as Arda and Luca approach.
- Use a low child-height tracking angle on the colourful stepping stones.
- Use a warm medium-wide shot containing Opa's chair, tea table, bookshelves and learning board together.
- Cut to close-ups of the three picture cards during the guessing exchange.
- Use a group three-shot for the vocabulary payoff.
- Follow Opa's wing as he reaches toward the empty basket location beside the shelf.
- Use quick but readable search shots around the chair, table and stump seats.
- End on a close-up of the red-and-white ribbon, then a reaction three-shot as they look toward the steps.

## Dialogue and Timing Rules
- No silent pause longer than approximately one second unless filled by an active search movement or clear facial reaction.
- Dialogue must overlap naturally with looking, pointing, walking and searching.
- Do not create a long establishing sequence before speech begins.
- Begin the first spoken line within the first 3–4 seconds.
- Keep the scene energetic enough for Reels while preserving Opa's calm personality.

## Sound and Music
- Light whimsical Pompom Hills music.
- Birds and gentle breeze in the huge canopy.
- Soft wooden footsteps on the platform and steps.
- Subtle book-close sound.
- Quiet porcelain sound from the tea set.
- Small musical discovery cue when Arda identifies the picnic blanket.
- Curious musical sting when the basket is found missing.
- Soft clue cue when Luca finds the ribbon.

## End Transition
The final frame shows Opa, Luca and Arda at the front edge of the platform, looking down toward the colourful stepping-stone path. Luca points toward the red-and-white ribbon near the steps. The next shot must begin from this exact location and continue the basket search without changing to a kitchen, cottage or unrelated interior.
