# English with Opa — Format Standard

---

## Series Identity

**English with Opa** is a story-driven English language learning series for preschool and early primary children (ages 3–6). Each episode follows Opa and one or two child characters through a warm, real-world mini-adventure. English is learned through context, action, and conversation — never through direct instruction or classroom-style repetition.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Story first, language second.** The adventure drives the scene. Language emerges naturally from what is happening.
2. **Opa teaches through doing.** He does not explain vocabulary. He models language through warm, natural conversation.
3. **Each shot is a mini-story.** Every shot has a beginning, middle, and end. It should work as a standalone Reel and as part of a larger episode.
4. **Dialogue feels conversational.** No scripted pronunciation exercises. No "Now repeat after me." Characters talk like real people in real situations.
5. **Learning focus is light.** 4–6 key expressions per shot, embedded in action.

---

## Episode Structure

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Shots per episode | 5–6 |
| Duration per shot | 40–60 seconds |
| Total episode length | 4–6 minutes |
| Characters per episode | Opa + 1–2 child characters |
| New location per shot | Preferred — each shot should feel visually distinct |
| Recurring object | One prop or object that carries through the whole episode (e.g. the picnic basket) |

---

## Shot Document Structure

Every shot document must include:

- **Purpose** — what this shot achieves in the story and for learning
- **Story Summary** — one short paragraph
- **Location / Environment** — visual and atmospheric description
- **Characters** — roles and personality notes
- **Visual Style** — animation style, mood, color
- **Duration** — target length
- **Emotional Beat** — the dominant feeling of the shot
- **Scene Flow** — numbered action steps
- **Dialogue** — complete with character labels
- **Learning Focus** — 4–6 key expressions
- **Camera Direction** — shot types and movement notes
- **Sound / Music** — ambient and musical notes
- **End Transition** — how this shot connects to the next
- **Important Notes** — production and tone reminders
- **OpenArt Prompt** — ready-to-use generation prompt

## Characters

| Character | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opa | Warm guide | Patient, wise, never lectures. Models language gently. |
| Arda | Active child | Energetic, eager, often acts first and thinks second. |
| Luca | Thoughtful child | Calmer, more observant, often the one who notices details. |
| Guest characters | Market sellers, neighbours, etc. | Appear for one shot only. Keep design simple and friendly. |

---

## Tone Rules

- Warm, safe, child-friendly at all times
- Small problems that children can relate to (lost items, wind, getting distracted)
- No danger, no conflict, no scolding
- Gentle correction only — Opa never raises his voice
- Resolution always comes through teamwork or kindness

---

## Language Rules

- All dialogue in natural English
- No Turkish, no code-switching
- No direct vocabulary instruction inside the video
- Key expressions appear naturally 1–2 times per shot
- Characters may repeat a phrase naturally (echo or confirmation) but never drill it
