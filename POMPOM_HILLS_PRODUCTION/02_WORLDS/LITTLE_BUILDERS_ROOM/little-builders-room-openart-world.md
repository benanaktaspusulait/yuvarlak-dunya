# Little Builders’ Room — OpenArt World Document

## World Name

**Little Builders’ Room**

## World Type

Reusable indoor preschool playroom and building area in Pompom Hills.

## Primary Purpose

Create one consistent, recognisable indoor world for calm construction play, toy-car challenges, shape activities, towers, simple problem-solving and cooperative play.

This is a permanent environment, not a single-episode set.

## Core Visual Identity

Little Builders’ Room is a warm, uncluttered preschool playroom with a large open floor at its centre. The room feels safe, cosy and thoughtfully handmade.

Its recognisable visual anchors are:

- one broad cream play rug with a subtle stitched scalloped border;
- a low rounded wooden storage wall along the back-left side;
- three open cubbies built into that storage wall;
- one small rainbow-shaped wall shelf above the cubbies;
- a wide arched window on the back-right wall;
- a soft mint-green reading bench beneath the window;
- one pale apricot feature wall;
- warm light-oak flooring;
- rounded corners and soft handcrafted forms throughout.

The centre of the rug must remain broad, flat and mostly empty so episode-specific play objects can be added later.

## Layout Lock

- Rectangular room with a clear, stable layout.
- Back wall: pale apricot.
- Side walls: warm cream.
- Floor: matte light oak with wide, simple planks.
- Large cream rug centred in the room.
- Low rounded storage unit fixed against the back-left wall.
- Rainbow wall shelf fixed above the storage unit.
- Wide arched window fixed on the back-right wall.
- Mint-green reading bench fixed directly beneath the window.
- Main doorway on the left wall, visible in the left-angle reference.
- The centre and front half of the room remain open.

Do not move, mirror, duplicate or redesign these anchors between reference images.

## Permanent Set Dressing

Keep permanent decoration minimal:

- two small fabric storage baskets inside separate cubbies;
- three simple wooden shape toys displayed inside one cubby;
- two small picture books resting on the reading bench;
- one tiny potted round-leaf plant placed high on the storage unit;
- simple cloud-shaped wall decoration near the window.

All permanent objects stay in the same position in every view.

## Episode Props — Exclude From World Build

Do **not** permanently include:

- the ten bridge-building blocks;
- toy car;
- start or finish cushions/mats;
- completed bridge;
- loose construction sets;
- character-specific toys;
- food, tables or chairs in the open play area;
- hobby horses;
- extra characters or animals.

These are story props and must be introduced only in the relevant episode shot.

## Materials and Colour

- Soft pastel preschool palette.
- Medium-low contrast.
- Matte handcrafted toy-set materials.
- Warm cream, pale apricot, muted mint, dusty sky blue and light natural wood.
- Soft fabric rug and baskets.
- No glossy plastic.
- No metallic shine.
- No hard reflections.
- No strong black outlines.
- No harsh shadows.
- No HDR appearance.
- Gentle, clean detail without excessive sharpness.

## Lighting

Warm daytime interior lighting.

Soft diffused daylight enters through the arched window from the back-right side. Add a subtle warm ambient fill so faces will remain readable anywhere on the rug.

The room must not contain:

- direct sunbeams;
- blown highlights;
- dramatic shadows;
- coloured stage lighting;
- night lighting;
- visible lamps acting as bright focal points.

## Scale

The environment must be correctly scaled for preschool children aged approximately four years.

- Storage unit height: approximately 0.75 m.
- Reading bench seat height: approximately 0.30 m.
- Doorway and window sized naturally relative to the children.
- Open play rug large enough for three children to sit or kneel around a construction activity without crowding.
- No oversized furniture or giant foreground objects.

## Camera Canon

- Child-eye camera height: approximately 0.80 m.
- Lens: 28–35 mm equivalent.
- Natural perspective.
- Straight vertical lines.
- No fisheye distortion.
- No extreme wide angle.
- No top-down security-camera look.
- No cinematic depth-of-field blur.
- The room should feel spacious but not cavernous.

## Continuity Rules for Future Shots

- The approved world must remain locked; do not regenerate or redesign it for later episodes.
- Use the world reference for environment identity and only the previous approved final frame as `@image1` for shot-to-shot continuity.
- Add episode props separately and preserve their inventory within that episode.
- Do not treat story props as permanent world objects.
- Keep the doorway, storage unit, window, bench, rug and decorations in their canonical positions.
- Preserve daytime lighting unless a separately approved night version is deliberately created.
- Prevent cumulative increases in contrast, saturation, brightness, HDR, glossiness and sharpness.
- Keep all camera movement slow, child-eye and spatially consistent.
